Looking for a great amp without emptying your wallet? Here are four that deliver serious sound and versatility for around $500 or less. The right one depends on how you play and where you'll use it: the bedroom, the studio, or a stage. Prices move around, so check current pricing before you buy.

## Boss Katana 100

The [Boss Katana](https://www.boss.info/) is a favorite for good reason. It packs a range of amp voicings and a full set of Boss effects into one combo that works at home, in the studio and on stage. The current generation is the newest, but a used MkII is still a great deal if you find one.

## Supro Delta King 8

Don't let the 1-watt rating fool you. The [Supro Delta King 8](https://suprousa.com/) is a little tube amp that gets surprisingly loud when you crank it, with gritty overdrive that blues and rock players love. Its small size and vintage look make it a great pick for home practice and recording.

## Orange Super Crush 100 Head

Need an amp that can handle gigs? The [Orange Super Crush 100](https://orangeamps.com/) has plenty of power and headroom for pedals, enough for bigger rooms and full-band volume. You get that signature Orange voice and can go from clean to high gain with ease. Just remember you'll need a cab.

## Positive Grid Spark

The [Positive Grid Spark](https://www.positivegrid.com/) is a smart practice amp loaded with amp models and effects. Its app can show chords for songs you're learning and generate backing tracks that follow your playing, which makes it a great practice partner when your bandmates aren't around.

## What really shapes your tone

Great tone isn't just about the gear. Here's what makes the biggest difference.

### Your hands

You are the biggest factor. A seasoned player can make a basic setup sound amazing, while a beginner can struggle with top-tier gear. Work on your technique and your feel first.

### Speakers and cab sims

For electric guitar, the speaker (or the impulse response, if you play direct) is the voice of your amp. Swapping speakers can change your tone more than swapping amps, so if you're upgrading, start there.

### Pickups

Single coils, humbuckers, active or passive: each has its own sound signature. Pick the type that suits your style and genre.

### Wood

Despite the endless forum debates, the wood in an electric guitar has a much smaller effect on tone than construction, pickups and setup. Don't stress about it.

Your gear shapes your sound, but your playing is the real game-changer. Experiment with pickups, speakers and settings until you find the tone that's yours.
