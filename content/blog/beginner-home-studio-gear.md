Setting up a home studio? Whether you're recording your first demo or finally upgrading from phone voice memos, the right gear makes a big difference. Here's what you need to get started, in roughly the order you should buy it.

## 1. A computer

Mac or PC both work. What matters is enough RAM and storage to run your DAW and hold your sessions. A recent MacBook or a solid Windows laptop is plenty for most home recording. One tip: you always need more storage than you think, so plan for an external drive.

## 2. A DAW (digital audio workstation)

Your DAW is where you record, edit and mix. Popular picks are [Ableton Live](https://www.ableton.com/), [FL Studio](https://www.image-line.com/) and [Logic Pro](https://www.apple.com/logic-pro/). Each has its own strengths, so try the free trials and pick the one that fits how you work. Logic Pro only runs on Apple devices.

## 3. An audio interface

The interface connects your mics and instruments to your computer and turns them into clean digital audio. The [Focusrite Scarlett 2i2](https://focusrite.com/products/scarlett-2i2) and the [PreSonus AudioBox](https://www.presonus.com/) are reliable starting points with two inputs, which covers a vocal and a guitar at the same time.

## 4. A microphone

You'll need a good mic for vocals and acoustic instruments. The [Audio-Technica AT2020](https://www.audio-technica.com/) is a great budget condenser, and the [Shure SM7B](https://www.shure.com/en-US/products/microphones/sm7b) is the classic all-rounder if you can stretch the budget. The SM7B needs a lot of gain, so check that your interface can drive it.

## 5. Studio monitors

Hearing your recordings accurately matters as much as capturing them. The [KRK Rokit 5](https://www.krkmusic.com/) is a solid budget pair, and the [Yamaha HS5](https://usa.yamaha.com/products/proaudio/speakers/hs_series/index.html) is famous for telling you the truth about your mix.

## 6. Headphones

For tracking, closed-back headphones like the [Beyerdynamic DT 770 Pro](https://www.beyerdynamic.com/) keep the click track out of your mic. For mixing, open-back headphones like the [Sennheiser HD 650](https://www.sennheiser-hearing.com/) give you a more natural picture.

## 7. A MIDI controller

To play virtual instruments and program drums, grab a small MIDI controller. The [Akai MPK Mini](https://www.akaipro.com/) is compact, cheap and does a lot for its size.

## 8. Acoustic treatment

Panels and bass traps cut down on reflections and boomy low end, so what you hear is closer to what you recorded. Brands like [Auralex](https://www.auralex.com/) and [Primacoustic](https://www.primacoustic.com/) are good places to look. This is a nice-to-have, not a must-have, so start with the gear above and treat the room once you know where the problems are.

## 9. A pop filter

It's the least exciting thing on this list, but a pop filter takes the harsh [plosives](https://thesoundofenglish.org/plosives/) out of vocals. Nothing fancy needed: the Nady MPF-6 is cheap and does the job.

## 10. Cables and stands

Don't forget the basics: decent XLR cables and a mic stand that doesn't droop mid-take. [Mogami](https://www.mogamicable.com/) cables and [On-Stage](https://on-stage.com/) stands are well-loved choices.

## Where to start

If you're on a tight budget, start with a computer you already own, a free or cheap DAW, a two-input interface, one good mic and a pair of closed-back headphones. That's enough to record real songs. Add monitors, a controller and room treatment as you go.
