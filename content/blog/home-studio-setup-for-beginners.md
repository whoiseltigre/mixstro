A good home studio setup for beginners is smaller and cheaper than most gear videos make it look. You don't need a wall of outboard gear to record a solid demo. You need a handful of pieces that work well together, bought in the right order.

Here's the gear that matters, starting with what you probably already own.

## The core of a home studio setup

### 1. A computer

Mac or PC, either works. What matters is enough RAM to run your sessions and enough storage to hold them. A recent laptop handles most home recording without trouble. One tip from experience: you always need more storage than you think, so plan on an external drive for projects and sample libraries.

### 2. A DAW

Your DAW (digital audio workstation) is where you record, edit and mix. The big three for beginners are [Ableton Live](https://www.ableton.com/), [FL Studio](https://www.image-line.com/) and [Logic Pro](https://www.apple.com/logic-pro/). All of them have free trials, so try two before you commit. Logic only runs on Apple devices, and GarageBand is a free way to learn the same workflow first.

### 3. An audio interface

The interface is the box that gets your mic and guitar into the computer. Two inputs are enough to start: one for a vocal mic, one for an instrument. The [Focusrite Scarlett 2i2](https://focusrite.com/products/scarlett-2i2) and [PreSonus AudioBox](https://www.presonus.com/) are reliable picks that a lot of people start on.

### 4. A microphone

If you only buy one mic, buy one that handles vocals and acoustic guitar well. The [Audio-Technica AT2020](https://www.audio-technica.com/) is a great budget condenser. The [Shure SM7B](https://www.shure.com/en-US/products/microphones/sm7b) is the classic all-rounder if you can stretch, but it needs a lot of gain, so check your interface can drive it.

## Hearing what you recorded

### 5. Studio monitors

Hearing your recordings accurately matters as much as capturing them. Regular speakers flatter your mix. Monitors tell you the truth. The [KRK Rokit 5](https://www.krkmusic.com/) is a solid budget pair, and the [Yamaha HS5](https://usa.yamaha.com/products/proaudio/speakers/hs_series/index.html) is famous for being honest, sometimes painfully so.

### 6. Headphones

You need closed-back headphones for tracking, so the click doesn't bleed into your mic. The [Beyerdynamic DT 770 Pro](https://www.beyerdynamic.com/) is a studio staple. If you end up mixing on headphones, open-backs like the [Sennheiser HD 650](https://www.sennheiser-hearing.com/) give a more natural picture.

## Nice to have, once you're recording

### 7. A MIDI controller

A small keyboard controller lets you play virtual instruments and program drums. The [Akai MPK Mini](https://www.akaipro.com/) is cheap, tiny and does a lot for its size.

### 8. Acoustic treatment

Panels and bass traps tame reflections and boomy low end, so your room stops lying to you. [Auralex](https://www.auralex.com/) and [Primacoustic](https://www.primacoustic.com/) are good places to look. Treat the room after you know where the problems are, not before.

### 9. A pop filter

The least exciting item here, but it takes the harsh [plosives](https://thesoundofenglish.org/plosives/) out of vocals. A cheap one like the Nady MPF-6 works fine.

### 10. Cables and stands

Decent XLR cables and a mic stand that doesn't droop mid-take. [Mogami](https://www.mogamicable.com/) cables and [On-Stage](https://on-stage.com/) stands are well-loved choices.

## What to buy first on a tight budget

Start with the computer you have, a free or cheap DAW, a two-input interface, one good mic and closed-back headphones. That's a complete home studio setup for recording real songs. Add monitors, a controller and room treatment as you go.

## FAQ

### How much does a beginner home studio cost?

You can put together a working setup for a few hundred dollars if you already own a computer: an interface, a mic, headphones and cables. Monitors and treatment roughly double that.

### Do I need studio monitors to start?

No. Good closed-back headphones are enough to start recording. Add monitors when you start mixing seriously.

### What's the best DAW for beginners?

The one you'll actually learn. GarageBand (Mac) and the free trials of Ableton Live and FL Studio are all good places to start. Recording a few songs teaches you more than any comparison chart.

### Can I record a full band at home?

You can, but you'll need an interface with more inputs and a room that can handle the volume. Many bands track drums somewhere else and do everything else at home. And if you're still [looking for a songwriting partner](/blog/best-songwriting-duos), a home studio is a great place to start writing together.
