Every guitarist has a list. Here's ours, split into legends, virtuosos, pioneers and modern players who are pushing the instrument forward.

## The legends

### Jimi Hendrix

Hendrix is widely considered the greatest guitarist in history, and it's hard to argue. His experimental approach changed how the guitar was played and what it could sound like. *Electric Ladyland*, the last studio album released in his lifetime, mixed blues, funk, jazz and heavy rock into something nobody had heard before. Start with "Purple Haze," "Hey Joe" and "Voodoo Child (Slight Return)."

### Eddie Van Halen

Van Halen's 1978 debut reset the bar for rock guitar. Two-hand tapping, dive bombs and fearless solos set Eddie apart, and "Eruption" made a generation of kids go buy a guitar. His playing on "Ain't Talkin' 'Bout Love," "Panama" and "Jump" showed he could write riffs as well as he could shred.

### Stevie Ray Vaughan

Stevie Ray Vaughan brought raw emotion and blistering technique back to the blues. *Texas Flood* and *Couldn't Stand the Weather* still sound urgent today, and "Pride and Joy" and "Crossfire" show how much groove he packed into his playing. ## The virtuosos

### Steve Vai

Steve Vai earned his reputation as the guitarist who can do anything, starting with Frank Zappa's band and moving on to David Lee Roth and Whitesnake. His album *Passion and Warfare* made him a guitar hero. He blends rock, blues and classical ideas with mind-bending technique and a strong sense of melody.

### Yngwie Malmsteen

The Swedish guitarist broke through with his debut solo album, *Rising Force*. Heavily influenced by J.S. Bach and Niccolò Paganini, Malmsteen fused classical music with metal, with lightning-fast arpeggios, sweep picking and scalloped fretboards. He more or less invented neoclassical metal.

## The pioneers

### Chuck Berry

Chuck Berry is one of the founding fathers of rock and roll guitar. He fused blues and country into fast, flashy electric playing, and the intro to "Johnny B. Goode" might be the most recognizable riff in rock and roll. Countless players picked up the guitar because of him.

### Nile Rodgers

Nile Rodgers shaped pop music as both a guitarist and producer. His tight, staccato funk rhythm with Chic powered "Le Freak" and "Good Times," and he went on to produce David Bowie's *Let's Dance* and play on Daft Punk's "Get Lucky." His rhythm playing proves you don't need a solo to be a guitar hero.

## Modern players to know

### Nita Strauss

Nita Strauss became the first woman with a signature Ibanez guitar in 2018. She's known for powerful, high-energy playing as Alice Cooper's guitarist, as well as her solo albums and her time in The Iron Maidens.

### Mateus Asato

Brazilian guitarist Mateus Asato built a huge following online with his fluid, melodic playing. He treats the electric guitar as a solo instrument, mixing chord melodies with lines that sound like singing, and he's become a big influence on a generation of players.

Who's on your list? The best part of a list like this is the arguments it starts at band practice.
