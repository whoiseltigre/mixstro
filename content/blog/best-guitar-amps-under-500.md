You don't need to spend a fortune to sound good. Some of the best guitar amps under $500 hold their own against gear that costs three times as much, as long as you pick the right one for where you play.

So start with that question. Is this for your bedroom, your home studio, or a stage with a drummer behind you? Here are four amps that cover all three. Prices move around, so check before you buy.

## Best all-rounder: Boss Katana 100

The [Boss Katana](https://www.boss.info/) is the amp a lot of players recommend first, and for good reason. You get a range of amp voicings and a full set of Boss effects in one combo that works at home, in the studio and on stage. The current generation is the newest, but a used MkII is still one of the best deals in guitar.

## Best for blues and recording: Supro Delta King 8

Don't let the 1-watt rating fool you. The [Supro Delta King 8](https://suprousa.com/) is a little tube amp that gets surprisingly loud when you crank it, with the kind of gritty breakup that the great [blues guitarists](/blog/best-blues-guitarists) built their sound on. It's small and looks the part, which makes it a favorite for home practice and recording.

## Best for gigs: Orange Super Crush 100 Head

Need an amp that can keep up with a loud drummer? The [Orange Super Crush 100](https://orangeamps.com/) has the power and headroom for bigger rooms and a pedalboard. It goes from clean to high gain easily, and that thick Orange voice suits rock and heavier styles, from stoner riffs to the [metalcore subgenres](/blog/metalcore-subgenres). Just remember it's a head, so budget for a cab.

## Best for practice: Positive Grid Spark

The [Positive Grid Spark](https://www.positivegrid.com/) is a smart practice amp packed with amp models and effects. Its app can show the chords for songs you're learning and generate backing tracks that follow your playing. It's a good practice partner for the nights your bandmates can't make it.

## How to choose between guitar amps under $500

- **Practice at home:** modeling amps like the Spark or Katana sound good quiet, which tube amps usually don't.
- **Recording:** a small tube amp like the Delta King, or an amp with a good direct output, fits easily into a [home studio setup](/blog/home-studio-setup-for-beginners).
- **Gigging:** you need power and headroom. A 100-watt head or the Katana 100 will hold up in a full band.

## What really shapes your tone

The amp is only part of the story. Here's what makes the biggest difference.

### Your hands

You're the biggest factor. An experienced player sounds great on basic gear, and the [best guitarists of all time](/blog/best-guitarists-of-all-time) could make almost anything sound like them. Work on your technique and feel first.

### Speakers and cab sims

The speaker (or the impulse response, if you play direct) is the voice of your amp. Swapping speakers can change your tone more than swapping amps, so it's the first upgrade to try.

### Pickups

Single coils, humbuckers, active or passive. Each has its own character, so match them to your style.

### Wood

Despite the forum debates, the wood in an electric guitar matters far less to tone than construction, pickups and setup. Don't lose sleep over it.

## FAQ

### Is a $500 amp good enough for gigs?

Yes. Plenty of working bands gig with amps in this range. Make sure it has enough power for the room, or plan to mic it through the PA.

### Tube or modeling amp?

Tube amps feel great and break up beautifully, but they need volume. Modeling amps give you more sounds and work at any volume. For most players starting out, modeling is the more practical choice.
